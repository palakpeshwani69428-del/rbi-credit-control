Qualitative Method — 02
Credit Rationing
📖 Definition
Credit Rationing is the method by which the RBI limits or controls the total amount of credit that commercial banks can extend to borrowers. RBI fixes the maximum credit limit for each bank or for specific sectors of the economy.
Two Forms of Credit Rationing
1
Variable Portfolio Ceiling
RBI fixes the maximum amount of credit that each commercial bank can provide — banks cannot lend beyond this ceiling
2
Variable Loan Ceiling
RBI fixes the maximum loan amount for specific purposes or sectors — e.g., maximum loan against commodities
Ensures credit flows to priority sectors like agriculture, MSMEs, and housing
Prevents over-concentration of credit in speculative or non-productive sectors
🎯 Priority Sectors Benefited
🌾 Agriculture
🏭 MSMEs
🏠 Housing
📚 Education
🌿 Renewable Energy
🏥 Healthcare
RBI mandates that 40% of Adjusted Net Bank Credit (ANBC) must go to priority sectors
Failure to meet targets results in banks contributing to RIDF (Rural Infrastructure Development Fund)
Effective tool during inflationary periods to prevent excess credit creation
💡 Key Insight: Credit rationing is both a credit control tool AND a development policy instrument — it simultaneously manages money supply and promotes inclusive economic growth.
