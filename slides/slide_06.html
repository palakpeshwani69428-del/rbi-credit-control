Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)
📖 Definition
CRR is the minimum percentage of total deposits that every scheduled commercial bank must maintain as cash with the Reserve Bank of India. Banks earn no interest on this reserve.
Formula
CRR = (Cash Reserve with RBI ÷ Net Demand & Time Liabilities) × 100
Maintained as cash — banks cannot use this for lending or investment
Directly controls the credit creation capacity of commercial banks
RBI can change CRR without prior notice — a powerful and immediate tool
Higher CRR = less money available for lending = tighter credit conditions

Of Net Demand & Time Liabilities (NDTL)
📈 CRR ↑
Banks hold more cash → Less funds for lending → Credit contracts → Inflation controlled
📉 CRR ↓
Banks hold less cash → More funds for lending → Credit expands → Growth stimulated
💡 Key Fact: A 1% change in CRR can inject or absorb approximately ₹1.5 lakh crore from the banking system, making it one of the most powerful monetary tools.
