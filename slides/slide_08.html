Repo Rate
📌 Full Form: Repurchase Agreement Rate
📖 Definition
Repo Rate is the rate at which the RBI lends short-term funds to commercial banks against the collateral of government securities, with an agreement to repurchase them at a predetermined price. It is the key policy rate of the RBI.
⚙️ How It Works
Banks sell government securities to RBI and receive funds → They agree to buy back the securities at a future date → The difference in price represents the Repo Rate interest
Repo Rate is always higher than Reverse Repo Rate
It is the benchmark rate — changes directly affect home loans, car loans, and all EMIs
Reviewed by the Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) at least 4 times a year
Current Repo Rate (2026)
5.25%
As per MPC Review — February 2026
📈 Repo Rate ↑ (Hawkish)
Borrowing from RBI becomes costly → Banks raise lending rates → Loans become expensive → Demand falls → Inflation controlled
📉 Repo Rate ↓ (Dovish)
Borrowing from RBI becomes cheaper → Banks lower lending rates → Loans become affordable → Demand rises → Growth stimulated
🏛 Policy Corridor: Repo Rate forms the middle of RBI's interest rate corridor, with MSF (5.50%) as the ceiling and SDF (5.00%) as the floor.
