Reverse Repo Rate
📖 Definition
Reverse Repo Rate is the rate at which the RBI borrows money from commercial banks — or equivalently, the rate at which banks park their surplus funds with the RBI. It is the opposite of the Repo Rate.
⚙️ How It Works
Banks deposit surplus funds with RBI → RBI pays interest at Reverse Repo Rate → Banks earn a safe return → Excess liquidity is absorbed from the system → Money supply decreases
Always lower than Repo Rate — forms the floor of RBI's interest rate corridor
When RBI raises Reverse Repo Rate, banks prefer to park funds with RBI rather than lend
Primary tool for absorbing excess liquidity from the banking system
Currently being replaced by the Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) as the floor rate

🎯 Key Purpose
When banks have excess funds, a higher Reverse Repo Rate incentivizes them to park money with RBI rather than lend, effectively reducing money supply and controlling inflation.
