Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR)
📖 Definition
SLR is the minimum percentage of Net Demand and Time Liabilities (NDTL) that every scheduled commercial bank must maintain in the form of liquid assets — cash, gold, or approved government securities — before providing credit to customers.
✅ Eligible Liquid Assets
💵 Cash
🥇 Gold
📜 Govt. Securities
🏛 Treasury Bills
📊 State Dev. Loans
Unlike CRR, SLR assets can be held by the bank itself (not deposited with RBI)
Banks earn returns on SLR assets (especially government securities)
Serves dual purpose: credit control AND ensuring bank solvency/stability
Higher SLR forces banks to invest more in govt. securities, reducing lending capacity
